Paying the balance of your 2025 Cyprus tax return

Whatever the 2025 return leaves to pay is paid in the Tax Department’s Tax Portal, by card or through internet banking with a payment reference code, by 31 October 2026. This is where the amount comes from, how each method works, and how to check that the money reached the right debt.

Who this is for

You need this if

  • You have filed, or are about to file, the 2025 return in TAXISnet and its Self-Assessment leaves income tax, GESY or defence contribution to pay.
  • The Tax Department has assessed a debt, or you entered a self-assessment in the Tax Portal yourself: the portal shows all of these in the same statement and they are paid the same way.
  • You have paid and want to check that the payment landed on the right debt, or you paid the wrong code, the wrong amount or twice.

You do not need this if

  • You are paying VAT, VIES charges or employer PAYE. The department routes those through Tax For All, not the Tax Portal.
  • You are self-employed with audited or reviewed accounts: the tax for 2025 had its own date, 1 August 2026. The deadlines guide has the calendar.
  • Your return shows a refund, not a balance. There is nothing to pay; what matters is the bank account the refund goes to.

What to prepare

  • Your TAXISnet username and password. The Tax Portal takes the same credentials, not the Tax For All or CY Login ones.
  • The 2025 return finally submitted, and its Self-Assessment figures to compare with what the portal shows.
  • A debit or credit card, or internet banking at one of the Cypriot banks the department lists.
  • A few minutes on the day you pay: the payment reference code is read from the portal, next to the debt it belongs to.

Steps

  1. 1

    Read the amount off the return

    The balance is what the return’s own Self-Assessment computes: the tax on the year’s income, less tax withheld by employers and amounts you already paid by self-assessment, which the Collection System enters in the return by itself. GESY and the defence contribution are computed in their own tables of the same return. Any GESY left in line 10B, even a few cents, is treated as due.

  2. 2

    Final Submit turns the balance into debts

    At Final Submit every amount to pay is created automatically in the Statement of Due Amounts of the Tax Portal, one line per tax code: 0300 income tax; 0315 GESY on employment, officers’ earnings and other income; 0313 GESY on self-employed profits; 0314 GESY on pensions; 0316 GESY on interest, dividends and rents; 0604 defence contribution on rents; 0612 on interest; 0613 on dividends. Nothing appears there before Final Submit.

  3. 3

    Sign in to the Tax Portal

    Go to taxportal.mof.gov.cy, choose Login, enter your TAXISnet username and password and the security code shown on the screen. A person you have authorised as your representative for the income tax return can manage your debts there with their own login.

    • Tax Portal (Φορολογική Πύλη) ↗
  4. 4

    Open the debt and find its payment reference code

    Choose Statement of Due Amounts and double-click the kind of tax — income tax, defence, GESY — to see the detail. Next to each debt is its payment reference code, a 13-digit number, with an icon for each of the two ways to pay. The amount shown includes the interest and charges accrued so far.

    • Tax Payments — the department’s step-by-step guide (English) ↗
  5. 5

    Pay by card

    The card icon, marked JCC Direct / JCC Smart, takes you to the card payment page. A card payment appears in the Tax Portal at once, so you can check it the same minute.

    • Tax Portal (Φορολογική Πύλη) ↗
  6. 6

    Or pay through internet banking

    In your bank’s internet banking choose Transfers and Payments → Tax Department and enter the payment reference code and the amount. Some banks accept the dashes in the code and some do not; if yours refuses it, type the 13 digits alone. The department’s guide lists Bank of Cyprus, Hellenic Bank, National Bank of Greece, Astrobank, Alpha Bank, Societe Generale, Eurobank, Ancoria Bank and RCB Bank. A bank payment appears in the Tax Portal after about three working days; keep your bank’s confirmation until it does.

    • Tax Payments — the department’s step-by-step guide (English) ↗
  7. 7

    Paying in several parts

    Use the same payment reference code for every part until that debt is cleared. By art. 39Α(1) of law 4/1978 each payment against a tax settles interest first, then the charges, and the tax itself last — so after the deadline a part-payment reduces the tax by less than its face value.

    • Tax Payments — the department’s step-by-step guide (English) ↗
  8. 8

    Check the payment and keep the proof

    Statement of Payments in the Tax Portal lists every payment and every set-off against your debts; the department names it as the place to verify a payment. If you used the wrong code, paid the wrong amount or paid the same thing twice, write to bankpayments@tax.mof.gov.cy with the payment details and a description of the mistake.

    • Οφειλές και Πληρωμές Φόρων — debts and payments (Greek) ↗

Where people go wrong

  • Looking for the debt before Final Submit. A return in temporary storage creates nothing in the Tax Portal.
  • Paying the whole balance under one reference. Each kind of tax — income tax, each GESY code, each defence code — has its own line and its own code; money sent under the income tax code does not settle the GESY.
  • A bank rejecting the reference because of the dashes. Enter the 13 digits alone.
  • Paying again because an internet-banking payment is not in the portal the next day. It takes about three working days to appear, and a double payment is one of the cases you then have to report to bankpayments@tax.mof.gov.cy.
  • Looking for an IBAN to wire the tax from abroad. The department’s payment pages describe two methods only: card from the Tax Portal and internet banking at the Cypriot banks it lists.
  • Signing in to the Tax Portal with the Tax For All password. It takes the TAXISnet credentials.
  • Assuming a part-payment after the deadline goes against the tax. It pays the interest and charges first.

Anything marked “practice” is what the department does in the office, not a rule you can hold it to. Rules carry a source.

Questions

Where do I pay the tax due under my 2025 Cyprus tax return?

In the Tax Department’s Tax Portal, taxportal.mof.gov.cy, signing in with your TAXISnet credentials. After Final Submit the debts appear under Statement of Due Amounts, each with a payment reference code, and you pay either by card from the portal or through internet banking at a Cypriot bank using that code.

What is the deadline for paying the 2025 balance?

31 October 2026, the same day as the return itself, under Κ.Δ.Π. 260/2026. A self-employed person with audited or reviewed accounts is excluded from that decree and had 1 August 2026 as the payment date for 2025. By the department’s announcement of 19 October 2026, a balance paid together with the return by 30 November 2026 carries no interest, no 5% and no €150; from 1 December the 5% attaches and interest counts from 1 November. The deadline itself stays 31 October.

I paid through my bank. Why is the payment not in the Tax Portal?

Internet-banking payments appear in the Tax Portal after about three working days; card payments appear at once. Check Statement of Payments after that time before paying again. If it still is not there, or you used the wrong code, write to bankpayments@tax.mof.gov.cy with the payment details.

What happens if I pay the 2025 tax late?

Under art. 50Α of law 4/1978 a charge of 5% of the tax is added once the deadline passes, and another 5% if it is still unpaid two months later. Interest runs as well, at the public default rate — 3.50% a year from 1 January 2026 — under art. 39. Part-payments settle the interest and charges before the tax.

Can I pay the balance in instalments?

You can pay a debt in several parts, using the same payment reference code each time until it is cleared, and the department’s guide says exactly that. Interest and the 5% charges still run on what is unpaid after the deadline. Art. 40 of law 4/1978 lets the Commissioner set instalment dates, but the department publishes no application procedure for a current-year balance.
